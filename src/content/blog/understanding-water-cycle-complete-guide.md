The water cycle, also known as the hydrological cycle, is one of Earth's most fundamental natural processes. Understanding this continuous movement of water is essential for students, teachers, and anyone interested in environmental science.

## What is the Water Cycle?

The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water within the Earth and atmosphere. It's a complex system that includes many different processes, but the four main stages are:

1. **Evaporation** - Water transforms from liquid to vapor
2. **Condensation** - Water vapor cools and forms clouds
3. **Precipitation** - Water falls back to Earth
4. **Collection** - Water gathers in bodies of water

## The Four Stages Explained

### 1. Evaporation

Evaporation is the process by which water changes from a liquid to a gas or vapor. The sun's heat provides energy for this transformation, primarily from:

- Oceans (which hold 97% of Earth's water)
- Lakes and rivers
- Soil and plants (transpiration)

**Key Facts:**
- About 90% of atmospheric water comes from ocean evaporation
- Transpiration from plants contributes another 10%
- Warm temperatures increase evaporation rates

### 2. Condensation

As water vapor rises into the atmosphere, it cools and transforms back into tiny water droplets. These droplets gather around dust particles, forming clouds.

**Key Facts:**
- Clouds form when air reaches its dew point
- Different cloud types form at different altitudes
- Condensation releases heat into the atmosphere

### 3. Precipitation

When cloud droplets combine and become too heavy, they fall back to Earth as precipitation. This can take several forms:

- **Rain** - Liquid water droplets
- **Snow** - Ice crystals
- **Sleet** - Ice pellets
- **Hail** - Large ice balls

**Key Facts:**
- Average global precipitation is about 990mm per year
- Precipitation varies greatly by region
- Mountains often receive more precipitation

### 4. Collection

Water that falls as precipitation collects in various locations:

- Oceans and seas
- Lakes and rivers
- Groundwater aquifers
- Glaciers and ice caps

**Key Facts:**
- 97% of Earth's water is in the oceans
- Only 3% is freshwater
- Most freshwater is locked in ice caps

## Why is the Water Cycle Important?

The water cycle is crucial for:

1. **Supporting Life** - All living things need water to survive
2. **Climate Regulation** - Water helps distribute heat around the planet
3. **Agriculture** - Farmers depend on predictable precipitation
4. **Ecosystem Health** - Water cycle maintains biodiversity

## Climate Change and the Water Cycle

Climate change is affecting the water cycle in several ways:

- **Increased evaporation** due to higher temperatures
- **More intense precipitation** events
- **Changed precipitation patterns** globally
- **Melting glaciers** affecting water supplies
