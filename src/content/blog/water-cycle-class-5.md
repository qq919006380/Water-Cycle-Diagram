# Water Cycle for Class 5: Complete Study Guide

The water cycle, also known as the **hydrological cycle**, is one of the most important natural processes on Earth. This guide will help Class 5 students understand all aspects of the water cycle.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Define the water cycle and its importance
- Explain the four main stages of the water cycle
- Understand how the sun drives the water cycle
- Describe different forms of precipitation

## What is the Water Cycle?

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. Water changes its state (liquid, gas, solid) as it moves through this cycle. The water cycle has been operating for billions of years and is essential for all life on Earth.

## The Four Main Stages

### Stage 1: Evaporation

**Definition:** Evaporation is the process by which water changes from a liquid to a gas (water vapor).

**How it works:**
- The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, rivers, and other water bodies
- Water molecules gain energy and escape into the atmosphere as water vapor
- About 90% of evaporation comes from oceans

**Key Points:**
- Evaporation happens faster at higher temperatures
- Wind increases the rate of evaporation
- Humidity affects evaporation rates

**Related Process - Transpiration:**
Plants release water vapor through tiny holes in their leaves called stomata. This process is called transpiration and contributes about 10% of atmospheric water vapor.

### Stage 2: Condensation

**Definition:** Condensation is the process by which water vapor cools and changes back into liquid water.

**How it works:**
- Warm, moist air rises into the atmosphere
- As air rises, it cools (temperature decreases with altitude)
- When air cools to its dew point, water vapor condenses
- Tiny water droplets form around dust particles, creating clouds

**Key Points:**
- Clouds are made of billions of tiny water droplets
- Different types of clouds form at different altitudes
- Condensation releases heat into the atmosphere

### Stage 3: Precipitation

**Definition:** Precipitation is any form of water that falls from clouds to the Earth's surface.

**Types of Precipitation:**

| Type | Description | Temperature |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Rain | Liquid water drops | Above 0C |
| Snow | Ice crystals | Below 0C |
| Sleet | Ice pellets | Near 0C |
| Hail | Large ice balls | Varies |

**How it works:**
- Water droplets in clouds collide and combine
- When droplets become too heavy, they fall as precipitation
- The type of precipitation depends on temperature

### Stage 4: Collection

**Definition:** Collection is the gathering of water in various locations after precipitation.

**Where water collects:**
- **Oceans and seas** - Hold 97% of Earth's water
- **Lakes and rivers** - Surface freshwater
- **Groundwater** - Water stored underground in aquifers
- **Glaciers and ice caps** - Frozen freshwater

**Runoff:** Water that flows over land surface into streams, rivers, and eventually the ocean.

**Infiltration:** Water that soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater.

## The Role of the Sun

The sun is the **primary energy source** that drives the water cycle:
- Provides heat energy for evaporation
- Creates wind patterns that move water vapor
- Affects global temperature and precipitation patterns

## The Role of Gravity

Gravity pulls water back to Earth:
- Causes precipitation to fall
- Makes rivers flow downhill
- Moves groundwater through soil and rock

## Importance of the Water Cycle

1. **Distributes freshwater** across the planet
2. **Regulates temperature** through heat transfer
3. **Supports all ecosystems** and life forms
4. **Shapes landscapes** through erosion and weathering
5. **Cleans water** through natural filtration

## Experiment: Create a Mini Water Cycle

**Materials needed:**
- Large bowl
- Small cup
- Plastic wrap
- Small weight (like a pebble)
- Warm water

**Steps:**
1. Pour warm water into the large bowl
2. Place the empty small cup in the center
3. Cover the bowl with plastic wrap
4. Place the weight on top of the plastic, directly over the cup
5. Place in sunlight and observe for 24 hours

**What happens:** Water evaporates, condenses on the plastic, and "rains" into the cup!

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Evaporation | Water changing from liquid to gas |
| Condensation | Water vapor changing to liquid |
| Precipitation | Water falling from clouds |
| Collection | Water gathering in bodies of water |
| Transpiration | Water released by plants |
| Runoff | Water flowing over land surface |
| Infiltration | Water soaking into ground |
| Water vapor | Water in gas form |
| Dew point | Temperature at which condensation occurs |

## Review Questions

1. What provides the energy for the water cycle?
2. Name the four main stages of the water cycle.
3. What is the difference between evaporation and transpiration?
4. Why does water vapor condense when it rises?
5. List three types of precipitation.
